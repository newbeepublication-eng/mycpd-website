A supervision agreement (sometimes called a supervision contract) is a short written record of how supervision will work between a supervisor and a supervisee. Agreeing it at the start makes sessions more focused, and it protects both of you if questions come up later.

Your employer may already have a supervision policy and template. If so, use that first. In the Irish public health service, for example, the HSE has issued *Supervision Guidelines for Health and Social Care Professionals* (Circular 02/2015). The list below covers what most good agreements include.

## 1. Who is involved

- Names, roles and contact details of the supervisor and supervisee
- The organisation or department, if relevant
- Who to contact if either person is unavailable for a long period

## 2. Purpose and type of supervision

Say what kind of supervision this is, because the focus changes:

- **Professional or clinical supervision**: reflecting on practice, cases and professional development
- **Line management supervision**: workload, performance and organisational issues
- **Peer supervision**: between colleagues at a similar level

Some agreements cover more than one type. If so, say how time is split.

## 3. Frequency, length and format

- How often (for example, monthly) and for how long (for example, 60 minutes)
- In person, online or by phone
- Where sessions take place, and that the space is private

## 4. Preparation and agenda

- Who sets the agenda, and how far in advance (for example, three days before)
- What the supervisee brings: cases to discuss, reflections, learning goals

## 5. Confidentiality and its limits

- What is discussed stays between you, **except** where there is a risk to a service user, a colleague or the public, or a serious concern about practice
- What happens in that case, and who would be told

## 6. Records

- Who writes the record of each session, and who gets a copy
- Where records are stored and for how long
- Keep records factual and avoid details that identify service users where possible
- Records should be handled in line with GDPR and your organisation's policy

## 7. Cancelling and rescheduling

- How much notice to give (for example, 48 hours)
- How quickly a missed session will be rearranged

## 8. Roles and expectations

- **Supervisor**: provide a safe space, give honest feedback, support learning
- **Supervisee**: come prepared, be open about difficulties, follow up on agreed actions
- Any fee, if supervision is private

## 9. If things are not working

- How either person can raise concerns about the supervision itself
- Who to go to if you cannot resolve it between you

## 10. Review and signatures

- When the agreement will be reviewed (for example, after six months, then every year)
- Signatures and dates from both people

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Names, roles and contact details
- [ ] Type and purpose of supervision
- [ ] Frequency, length, format and location
- [ ] Agenda and preparation
- [ ] Confidentiality and its limits
- [ ] Record keeping
- [ ] Cancellation and rescheduling
- [ ] Roles and expectations, and any fee
- [ ] What to do if supervision is not working
- [ ] Review date and signatures

## Does supervision count as CPD?

It can, when it leads to new or enhanced learning that you reflect on. A session spent only on caseload admin usually will not count. Note what you learned and how it changes your practice, as you would for a course, and check your own regulator's guidance.
