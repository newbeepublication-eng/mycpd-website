If you are registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) in the UK, you sign a declaration at every renewal that you meet the standards for continuing professional development (CPD). A small number of registrants are then asked to prove it.

## How HCPC audits work

- Registrants **renew every two years**, and CPD is linked to renewal.
- At each renewal, the HCPC randomly selects **2.5 per cent of each profession** to submit a CPD profile.
- You can only be audited if you have been registered for **two years or more**. Recent graduates are not selected at their first renewal, and neither are people returning from a break in registration.
- Selection is computer-generated, so you could be selected more than once.
- If you are selected, the HCPC sends you information on completing your profile. If you have unavoidable circumstances, you can ask to **defer**. If your profile is found not to meet the standards, you can **appeal**.

## The five CPD standards

Your profile is assessed against these five standards. A registrant must:

1. **Maintain a continuous, up-to-date and accurate record of their CPD activities**
2. **Demonstrate that their CPD activities are a mixture of learning activities relevant to current or future practice**
3. **Seek to ensure that their CPD has contributed to the quality of their practice and service delivery**
4. **Seek to ensure that their CPD benefits the service user**
5. **Upon request, present a written profile (which must be their own work and supported by evidence) explaining how they have met the standards for CPD**

## How to meet each standard in practice

**1. A continuous record.** Log CPD as it happens, not in a rush before renewal. Gaps of many months are easier to spot than you might think.

**2. A mixture of activities.** Courses are only one kind of CPD. Include work-based learning, reading, reflective practice, supervision, teaching others and professional activity. Variety shows a rounded approach to learning.

**3. Quality of practice and service delivery.** For your main activities, write down what changed afterwards: a new assessment you now use, a process you improved, a guideline you introduced to your team.

**4. Benefit to service users.** Connect your learning to the people you work with. Keep it anonymous, for example "children on my caseload" rather than names or identifying details.

**5. Your own work, with evidence.** Write the profile yourself. Keep evidence for each activity you describe, such as certificates, reflective notes, feedback or documents you produced.

## A simple routine

- After each activity, spend five minutes noting **what you learned** and **what you will do differently**.
- Keep the evidence with the entry straight away.
- Every few months, check that your record shows a **mix** of activities.
- Before renewal, pick the few activities with the clearest benefit to service users. These make the strongest examples if you are audited.
